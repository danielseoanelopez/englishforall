MISSION 3 · THE THREE GRIDS

Talk through each row before you reveal it. What do you remember? Then compare: “In the first film…, whereas in Ares…”

TRON1982 · dir. Steven LisbergerTRON: LEGACY2010 · dir. Joseph KosinskiTRON: ARES2025 · dir. Joachim Rønning

MISSION 4 · GOING DEEPER

Choose questions from each tier. Push for reasons and examples, not just yes or no.

TIER 1 · THE FILMS
  1. Which film has aged the best, and which feels the most dated? Why?
  2. Ares was the first Tron film where the hero is a program, not a human. Did that make you care more or less?
  3. Compare the villains. Who was the most threatening: the MCP, Clu or Julian Dillinger?
  4. Each film has a very different soundtrack. How much does the music shape the mood?
TIER 2 · THE IDEAS
  1. In 1982 the fear was being trapped inside a computer. In 2025 it's AI coming out into our world. What does that change tell us about each era?
  2. Programs in Ares last only 29 minutes in the real world. Is a built-in limit like that a smart safeguard for AI, or a kind of cruelty?
  3. Ares starts to question his orders. Can something that was programmed ever really have free will?
  4. Is it fair to compare a 1982 film with a 2025 one, or should each be judged by the standards of its time?
TIER 3 · REAL LIFE
  1. Who should own powerful technology: big companies, governments or nobody? Use Eve Kim and Julian Dillinger as examples.
  2. Which ideas from the Tron films have actually come true since 1982?
  3. Would you want a digital version of yourself to keep existing after you die? Why, or why not?
  4. Sequels and reboots dominate cinema now. Is that a good thing for audiences?

MISSION 5 · BOSS BATTLE: THE PERMANENCE CODE

The code that lets programs live permanently in the real world has just been found. Pick a side and argue for two minutes, then swap. Use at least three toolkit phrases.

Release it

Permanent programs could transform medicine, rebuild infrastructure and end food shortages.

  • Think of the people it could help.
  • Progress always comes with risk.
  • If we don't, someone worse will.

Destroy it

Permanent programs could be turned into soldiers that never stop and can't be switched off.

  • Who controls them?
  • Some technology is too dangerous to exist.
  • The 29-minute limit is there for a reason.

▸ TEACHER NOTES

About 60 minutes. Suggested timing: boot sequence 10, toolkit intro 5, three grids 15, going deeper 15, boss battle 10, ranking 5.

  • Keep the toolkit visible all session and tap phrases as they come up. Aim for 10 or more by the end.
  • Correct after the speaking, not during. Note errors and review them at the end.
  • Push for linked answers: opinion, then reason, then example.
  • Common errors to listen for: “more better”, “the same than” (the same as), “different of” (different from), and “whereas” used to start a new sentence on its own.
  • Homework option: a short review comparing two of the films, 150–200 words.
