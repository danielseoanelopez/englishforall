    Article: “The plan that taught me the most”

    A careers magazine has asked readers to write about a plan, project or goal that went wrong. Describe what happened, explain what caused it, and say what you learned.

    • 180–220 words, in three or four paragraphs
    • Use 6 or 7 of the 10 phrasal verbs, in different tenses. More is not better: a good writer chooses them where they sound natural.
    • Give your article an engaging title
    • Use a semi-formal, reflective tone

    ▸ TEACHER: SAMPLE ANSWER (7 VERBS)

    The Launch That Never Was

    Two years ago, I was asked to lead the launch of a mobile app for a small travel company. On paper, it looked simple. In reality, the whole project ended up falling through, and it taught me more than any success ever has.

    Looking back, most of our problems stemmed from poor communication. The developers and the marketing team rarely spoke, and whenever someone raised a concern, I played it down because I didn't want to come across as negative. We kept telling ourselves we would iron out the issues later.

    Three weeks before launch, our main investor withdrew. At first we couldn't rule out a rescue deal, but it soon became clear that the company would have to cancel the project entirely.

    What did I learn? Above all, that a leader has to stay firm under pressure, even when it means delivering bad news early. Honest conversations bring about better decisions than polite silence ever will.

    In the end, I think good leadership is really about one thing: dealing with small problems before they become big ones.
